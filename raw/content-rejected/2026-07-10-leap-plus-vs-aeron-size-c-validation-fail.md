    <h2>Height-Bracket Verdicts</h2>

    <h3>6'2" – 6'4": Aeron Size C Has the Edge</h3>
    <p>At this height range, popliteal height (back of knee to floor) typically falls between 17.5" and 19.5". The Aeron Size C's seat height ceiling of 21.5" covers that range comfortably with room to raise the seat for standing-desk alternation. More importantly, the back height of 24.75" positions the PostureFit SL pads at the correct sacral and lumbar zones for a torso in this bracket — the engineering reason is that lumbar lordosis support needs to contact L3–L5 vertebrae, which in a 6'3" user typically sit 8"–10" above the seat pan. The Aeron's fixed geometry hits that target without manual guessing. Tall users in r/ergonomics report that mesh back and seat breathability is a primary reason for choosing the Aeron, and at 6'2"–6'4" where fit is confirmed, breathability becomes the tiebreaker for all-day work. Note: if your thigh is longer than 23", the Aeron's fixed 16.75" seat depth may be too shallow — use the <a href="/knee-pain-seat-depth/" class="link-internal">seat depth calculator</a> to verify before buying.</p>

    <h3>6'4" – 6'6": Leap Plus Takes It</h3>
    <p>Above 6'4", two fitment problems compound: the seat height needed to clear a popliteal height of 20"+ starts pushing the Aeron Size C toward its ceiling, and thigh length commonly exceeds 24", making the Aeron's fixed mesh seat depth genuinely inadequate. The Leap Plus sliding seat pan adjusts from 15.5" to 18.5" — at 6'5" with a 24.5" thigh, the target depth is approximately 17.5"–18", which the Leap Plus covers cleanly. From a mechanical standpoint, seat pan pressure distribution under a tall femur is a function of contact area; a seat too shallow concentrates load behind the knee and at the ischial tuberosities simultaneously, which is the root cause of the thigh numbness tall users frequently report. The Leap Plus also carries a 500 lb weight capacity, relevant for taller users whose body weight is proportionally higher. Tall users report the foam seat and padded back combination is noticeably more comfortable than mesh after long sessions — one owner described switching from a gaming chair as a dramatic improvement. See the full <a href="/review/leap-plus/" class="link-internal">Leap Plus review</a> for detailed spec analysis.</p>

    <!-- LUMBAR ENGINEERING SECTION -->
    <h2>Lumbar Support Engineering: Why the Mechanism Matters at 6'+</h2>
    <p>Both chairs use different mechanical approaches to lumbar support, and for tall users the distinction is clinically relevant. The Aeron's PostureFit SL uses two independent pads — one targeting the sacrum, one the lumbar — connected by a rigid bridge. This fixed geometry delivers consistent support angle (approximately 10–15 degrees of anterior pelvic tilt encouragement), but it cannot shift vertically. At 6'4", the lumbar pad may contact L4–L5 correctly; at 6'6", it may ride too low toward the sacrum. The Leap Plus uses Steelcase's Live Back system, where the entire backrest flexes laterally and vertically with the user's movement, combined with a height-adjustable lumbar pad. The engineering reason this matters is that a tall user shifting forward to read a screen will change their lumbar curvature — a rigid back system at that point becomes a point-load rather than distributed support. For users above 6'4" who move frequently throughout the day, the adaptive compliance of the Leap Plus back is a mechanical advantage. Compare both against the Gesture in our <a href="/gesture-vs-leap-plus/" class="link-internal">Gesture vs Leap Plus comparison</a>.</p>

    <!-- SEAT MATERIAL TRADEOFFS -->
    <h2>Mesh vs. Foam: The Tall-User Tradeoff</h2>
    <p>The Aeron runs full mesh on both the seat and back. Mesh breathability is real and significant — tall users in r/ergonomics consistently cite it as a primary reason for the Aeron choice, particularly in warmer office environments. However, at least one long-term Aeron owner reported that the mesh seat subtly tilts the pelvis over time, flattening the lumbar curve and contributing to low back issues after extended use — the mesh tension sags and the hammock geometry shifts load distribution in ways foam does not. The Leap Plus foam seat maintains consistent pressure distribution as long as the foam is in good condition, which Steelcase's 12-year warranty covers. For tall users, the foam seat also allows a firmer edge that prevents the "hammocking" effect that a heavy user can induce in mesh over time. If your priority is temperature regulation and you're in the confirmed fit zone (6'2"–6'4"), the Aeron mesh is a real benefit. If you're above 6'4" and sitting 8+ hours daily under load, the Leap Plus foam is the lower-risk long-term choice. For more on how seat depth interacts with these materials, see the <a href="/knee-pain-seat-depth/" class="link-internal">knee pain and seat depth guide</a>.</p>

    <h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

    <h3>Is the Steelcase Leap Plus better than the Aeron Size C for tall users?</h3>
    <p>It depends on height bracket. For users between 6'2" and 6'4", the Aeron Size C edges ahead due to its superior seat height ceiling of 21.5" and a back height of 24.75" that positions lumbar support correctly for longer torsos. Above 6'4", the Leap Plus gains an advantage with its broader adjustable seat pan (15.5"–18.5" depth), higher weight capacity (500 lbs vs 350 lbs), and adaptive Live Back system that better accommodates the movement patterns of very tall users.</p>

    <h3>What is the seat height range of the Aeron Size C?</h3>
    <p>The Herman Miller Aeron Size C has a seat height range of approximately 16"–21.5", making it one of the higher-reaching standard office chairs on the market. Tall users with inseams above 34" will typically need the upper end of this range to achieve a 90-degree knee angle without thigh compression against the seat pan edge.</p>

    <h3>Does the Steelcase Leap Plus have enough seat depth for a 6'4" person?</h3>
    <p>Based on specs, yes. The Steelcase Leap Plus offers a seat depth range of approximately 15.5"–18.5" with a sliding seat pan. At 6'4" with a typical thigh length of 24"–25", the adjustable seat pan can be extended to support the thigh without cutting off circulation behind the knee. The target depth for that anthropometry is 17"–18", which falls within the Leap Plus range. Use the <a href="/knee-pain-seat-depth/" class="link-internal">seat depth calculator</a> to verify your specific measurement before purchasing.</p>

    <h3>Which chair has a higher weight capacity, the Leap Plus or the Aeron Size C?</h3>
    <p>The Steelcase Leap Plus is rated for up to 500 lbs, specifically engineered for higher-load users. The Herman Miller Aeron Size C is rated for 350 lbs. For tall users who also carry significant body weight, the Leap Plus frame geometry and structural tolerances make it the safer fitment choice from a load-rating standpoint. The engineering reason is that tall users apply force at a longer lever arm from the seat center, which increases effective load on the base and tilt mechanism even at equivalent body weight.</p>
