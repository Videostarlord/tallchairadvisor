## 3. Technical SEO Issues

### What is working well

- HTTPS is enforced with `301` from `http://` to `https://`.
- Canonical URLs are self-referencing on all 33 canonical pages.
- Robots and XML sitemap are both present and valid.
- Brotli compression is enabled on HTML.
- Cloudflare CDN is active (`server: cloudflare`, `cf-cache-status`).
- TLS 1.2 and TLS 1.3 are available; TLS 1.0 and 1.1 are rejected.
- Security headers are strong: `Strict-Transport-Security`, `Content-Security-Policy`, `X-Frame-Options`, `X-Content-Type-Options`, `Referrer-Policy`, and `Permissions-Policy` are present.
- No `noindex` directives were found on canonical content pages.

### Issues

#### Critical

- No true critical crawl/indexation blockers were found.

#### High

- Problem: Broken internal email-obfuscation URL on `/contact/` points to `/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection`, which returns HTTP 404 to crawlers.
  Why it hurts SEO: Search crawlers can hit a dead internal URL. It is a minor crawl-quality problem and looks like a broken internal link in audits.
  How to fix it: Replace the crawlable obfuscation link with a rendered `mailto:` link, plain text email, or a server-side obfuscation method that does not expose a dead href.
- Problem: 14 unique internal URLs use slashless paths and require a 308 hop to the canonical trailing-slash URL.
  Why it hurts SEO: This wastes crawl budget, adds latency, and introduces avoidable redirect hops in internal linking.
  How to fix it: Standardize all internal links, nav items, and CTA targets to the canonical trailing-slash version.
- Problem: Estimated JS weight is dominated by `https://www.googletagmanager.com/gtag/js?id=G-TWK4EPV8DT` at about 459 KB on every page.
  Why it hurts SEO: Even async third-party JS adds transfer cost and can increase INP/network contention, especially on mobile.
  How to fix it: Audit whether full `gtag.js` is needed on every page, defer nonessential tracking, or switch to a lighter analytics setup.

#### Medium

- Problem: Homepage `WebSite` schema advertises a `SearchAction` to `/?q={search_term_string}`, but `?q=` returns the homepage rather than a real search results page.
  Why it hurts SEO: Invalid or misleading structured data can reduce schema trust and create validation warnings.
  How to fix it: Remove `SearchAction` until a true search endpoint exists, or implement a search results page that resolves the query.
- Problem: The `/chairs/...` support cluster is reachable, but several child URLs have weak prominence: 2 pages sit at depth 3 and many have only 2 to 8 internal inlinks.
  Why it hurts SEO: Lower prominence makes it harder for long-tail support pages to accumulate authority and rank consistently.
  How to fix it: Add contextual links from reviews, comparison pages, and the best-chairs hub to seat-height, seat-depth, weight-limit, and fit-guide subpages.
- Problem: All 33 canonical pages are missing `twitter:image` even though Twitter cards are declared.
  Why it hurts SEO: This weakens social previews and can reduce click-through from shared links.
  How to fix it: Add `twitter:image` for every template, ideally matching the existing `og:image`.
- Problem: Two key pages have overlong meta descriptions: `/office-chairs-for-tall-people/` (175 chars) and `/chairs/steelcase-gesture/` (164 chars).
  Why it hurts SEO: Long descriptions are more likely to truncate in SERPs and can lose the strongest CTA or differentiator.
  How to fix it: Trim both to roughly 140 to 155 characters while keeping the core keyword and value proposition.
- Problem: The LinkedIn profile URL used in the About page and Person schema ends with a trailing hyphen and should be verified manually.
  Why it hurts SEO: If the profile URL is wrong, it weakens `sameAs` entity signals and sends users to a dead or blocked profile.
  How to fix it: Confirm the exact LinkedIn vanity URL and update both the visible link and `sameAs` property.
- Problem: Some authority citations point to generic resource hubs like PubMed root instead of specific studies.
  Why it hurts SEO: Generic references are weaker trust signals than precise source citations tied to the claim being made.
  How to fix it: Link directly to the specific studies or standards that support each ergonomics claim and cite the study title/year in the page copy.

#### Low

- Problem: I did not detect visible breadcrumb UI in sampled article/review templates, even though breadcrumb schema is present on 29 pages.
  Why it hurts SEO: Visible breadcrumbs add internal links, improve orientation, and reinforce hierarchy for users and crawlers.
  How to fix it: Add a small HTML breadcrumb component above the article/review heading.
- Problem: `/contact/`, `/privacy-policy/`, and `/affiliate-disclosure/` have no JSON-LD.
  Why it hurts SEO: These pages are still crawlable and indexable, but they miss easy structured-data enhancements.
  How to fix it: Add minimal `WebPage` or `ContactPage`/`AboutPage` schema where appropriate.
- Problem: HTML pages use a short edge cache window (`Cache-Control: public, max-age=3600, must-revalidate`).
  Why it hurts SEO: This is not an SEO blocker, but a longer edge TTL could reduce TTFB volatility on repeat requests.
  How to fix it: If the publishing workflow allows it, increase HTML cache efficiency with stale-while-revalidate or longer CDN edge caching.
- Problem: Query-parameter URLs like `/?q=test` and `/?utm_source=test` return `200` with a canonical back to the root rather than a hard redirect.
  Why it hurts SEO: Canonical tags control duplication, but redirecting junk parameters is a cleaner crawl-budget solution.
  How to fix it: Normalize or strip nonfunctional parameters at the edge when possible.
- Problem: Amazon links returned `503` and LinkedIn returned `999` to this crawler environment.
  Why it hurts SEO: This is probably bot mitigation rather than a dead link, but it is worth verifying manually in a browser.
  How to fix it: Spot-check affiliate and profile links manually to confirm they resolve for human users.

- Estimated heaviest pages by total transfer footprint:

- If the three product images on `/best-office-chairs/` are above the fold on desktop, their lazy-loading strategy may delay perceived paint.
