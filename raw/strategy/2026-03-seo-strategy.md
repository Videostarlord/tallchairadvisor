## Niche Defensibility Analysis

### Why this niche works
1. **Underserved demand** — Searches like "best chair for 6'4"" and "steelcase gesture seat height for tall people" have real volume with few authoritative pages
2. **High purchase intent** — Tall people have genuinely bad options; they research carefully before a $700–$2,000 purchase
3. **High AOV** — Ergonomic chairs are expensive. Even 1–2% commission on a $1,200 chair = $12–24/sale
4. **Spec-driven content** — Height-specific ergonomic specs (seat height range, seat depth, back height) are verifiable facts that build trust and resist AI summary displacement
5. **First-mover advantage in sub-niche** — Competitors like BTOD focus on weight capacity; Tall.Life covers broader lifestyle; no one owns height-specific chair ergonomics

### Moat-building opportunities
- Proprietary fit guides with exact measurements (seat height for 6'1" vs 6'4" vs 6'7")
- User-generated data (future: survey tall readers for real-world fit feedback)
- Relationships with brands for early review units
- Community presence on tall-people subreddits (r/tall, r/BTFC)

## AI Search Readiness (GEO)

### Why this matters now
Perplexity, ChatGPT search, and Google AI Overviews are increasingly citing niche, spec-accurate sources. TCA's precise height-specific data is ideal citation material if the site is structured for it.

### Action items
- [ ] Add `llms.txt` at root (whitelist GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot)
- [ ] Write passage-level, self-contained paragraphs — e.g., "The Steelcase Gesture has a seat height range of 15.5"–20.5", making it one of the tallest-adjusting chairs available."
- [ ] Add FAQ schema (FAQPage) to all review pages
- [ ] Ensure every spec claim is attributed and verifiable (link to manufacturer spec sheet)
- [ ] Structure "For tall people verdict" sections as standalone paragraphs AI can lift
- [ ] Consider a "Quick Answer" box at the top of each review (targets featured snippets + AI summaries)
